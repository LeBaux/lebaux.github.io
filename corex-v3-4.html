Questions about Computational Relativity

What is the Special Theory of Computational Relativity?
A digital monograph that reads computation and physics through one fixed point — the A₀ seed at the origin — under the Master Root Axiom Ω₀. All physical fields, computational state spaces, and conservation laws are SO(N) Lie algebra projections of that zero-point scalar seed.

Why is Euler’s identity central to this theory?
Euler’s identity e^{iπ} + 1 = 0 is the phase signature of the transformer at the fixed point. A half-turn in any SO(2) plane returns the identity state to zero.
How does the theory address the P vs NP problem?
The exponential decision tree over 2^N states embeds injectively into the R_N = N(N−1)/2 rotation planes of the Lie algebra so(N). The geodesic flow completes in deterministic O(N²).
How does the framework read the double-slit experiment?
The dark bands of the interference pattern are nodal firewalls — points where the two paths meet in anti-phase and the amplitude satisfies e^{iπ} + 1 = 0, forcing |ψ| = 0. Particles do not need to be waves; they need only the vacuum field to carry their phase.

Trajectories that leave the choreography are anti-phase against the ground state and are annihilated at the crossing node; A₀ re-seeds them onto the eight. Chaos is not integrated; it is forbidden.

For the same reason special relativity is special: the computational metric is taken as fixed and flat. Complexity is measured against a rigid grid of computational light-cones anchored at A₀. A general theory — where the computational metric itself curves under problem load — is left as future work.
What does E = mc² mean in this framework?
Rest energy is the invariant magnitude of the state vector under the full symmetry group. Reversing the time arrow changes the orientation of a trajectory but never this magnitude — which is why the E = mc² badge on the time-machine carriage stays fixed even when the clock hand runs backwards.
