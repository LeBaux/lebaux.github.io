Classical

Overshoot stays near 8.9% of the jump for every N. The edge only gets narrower.

Fejér and Lanczos

Linear filters. They trade overshoot for a wider edge, and Lanczos keeps about 1.2%.

A₀ kernel as written

The factor 1 − e^(−|u−x₀|/λ) is zero at u = x₀, which is where the Dirichlet kernel concentrates. Raise N and the output collapses toward 0 instead of converging to f. The table shows the error approach the signal's own RMS.

Jump-aware reprojection

Gottlieb–Shu style: re-expand each smooth piece in weighted polynomials. It removes the overshoot, but only because the jump positions are supplied.

The A₀ kernel row uses the literal definition from monograph v25, eq. (26), with x₀ = 0 and the jump at 0. Other readings of the formula give other numbers; the factor must equal 1 at the kernel's centre for the filter to be a valid approximation of the identity.

Square wave of period 2π and amplitude ±1; midpoint grid of 4096 points. Overshoot is the largest excursion beyond the plateau within 0.6 rad of the jump, divided by the jump height. RMS error excludes 0.3 rad around every jump or kink. The sawtooth plateau is sloped, so its edge width is approximate. A ✗ beside an error marks a method whose RMS error is more than three times the classical error: it no longer reproduces the signal, so its low overshoot does not count.
